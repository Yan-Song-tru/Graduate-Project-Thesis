# 7. Suggested Proposal Timeline

The following is an example timeline for the first three to four months. I use it as a planning guide, not as a set of rigid deadlines. We can adjust it based on your background, the pathway you choose, and how the research develops.

| Period | Main goal | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1-2 | Orientation and broad topic selection | Initial research area |
| Weeks 3-5 | Background reading | Key-paper list and reading notes |
| Weeks 6-8 | Structured literature review | Literature map or table and candidate gaps |
| Weeks 9-10 | Topic refinement | Research question or questions |
| Weeks 11-12 | Initial methodology | Proposed approach and evaluation plan |
| Weeks 13-14 | Proposal drafting | First complete draft |
| Following weeks | Revision | Proposal ready for submission or presentation |

Research does not always progress linearly. A promising research question may turn out to be infeasible or already well studied, and you may need to return to the literature and refine the direction. This is a normal part of research, not a sign that the process has failed.
