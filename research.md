# 3. Research Interests and Potential Projects

## Research interests

My current research interests include topics that solve the foundational problems relevant to potential real-world applications in health, behaviour analysis, and intelligent systems. For example, some ideas ask: "can we build a system that understands what people are doing, when they are doing it, and how to do so with limited or imperfect data?"

- Computer vision and video understanding: teaching machines to interpret images and video, from tracking a person in a room to recognising subtle movements in a rehabilitation exercise.
- Human action recognition and temporal action detection: identifying what action is occurring and when it starts and ends in a video. For example, recognising that a person is walking, sitting, or falling, and finding the exact time segment where that action occurs.
- Multimodal learning: combining different kinds of information, such as RGB data, skeleton data, depth data, or health signals, to make a more reliable prediction than using only one source.
- Skeleton-based human activity recognition: representing a person as a set of key body joints or poses, rather than processing raw video frames directly. This can be useful when the focus is on posture, movement patterns, or gait, and when privacy becomes a concern.
- Learning with limited annotations: designing methods that can still learn useful patterns when labelled data are scarce, expensive, or time-consuming to collect.
- Weakly supervised and semi-supervised learning: learning from a small number of labelled examples or from only coarse labels, such as knowing a video contains an activity but not exactly where it occurs in time.
- Continual and incremental learning: enabling a system to learn new information over time without forgetting what it has already learned, which is important for evolving real-world settings.
- Deep learning applications in healthcare and human behaviour analysis: using learning-based methods to analyse patient movement, daily activities, recovery progress, or behavioural patterns from data collected in clinics, homes, or hospitals.
- Machine learning in health: applying machine learning to health-related questions such as risk prediction, patient monitoring, or treatment response.
- Other topics that connect naturally with these areas: this can include representation learning, robust modelling, signal processing, or trustworthy AI methods that support these applications.


## Potential MSc projects

I usually do not begin with a fixed list of thesis topics. Instead, we develop projects at the intersection of my research program, your interests and background, and a research gap or practical opportunity that we identify through reading and discussion.

A project may therefore begin with a broad direction rather than a fully specified title. We can narrow it as we learn more about what has already been done, what remains unresolved, and what is realistic with the available data and computing resources.

The eventual project or thesis topic should be grounded in the literature and in a clear understanding of what we can investigate well. An initial conversation is often enough to identify a promising direction; the details can develop from there.
