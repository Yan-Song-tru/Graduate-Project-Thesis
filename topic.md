# 5. Choosing a Research Topic

You do not need to arrive with a fully formed thesis or project topic. I expect topic development to be gradual: as you learn the field, test your assumptions, and discuss possible directions with me, you will develop a clearer sense of which questions are meaningful and feasible.

## The two options

### Thesis option

A thesis is the more research-intensive pathway. It is built around a clearly motivated research question, a substantial literature review, and a systematic investigation that aims to make a defensible contribution or produce new understanding. You will typically develop and evaluate a method, analyze results critically, and present the work in a thesis and defense.

### Project option

A project is usually more applied and more tightly scoped. It may focus on implementing, adapting, comparing, or evaluating existing methods or systems for a defined problem, with an emphasis on a useful technical outcome and clear documentation. A project can still involve substantial experimentation and analysis, but it does not necessarily require the same level of novelty or research contribution as a thesis.

### Choosing between them

Both options require careful topic selection, background reading, planning, and sustained independent work. We should discuss the pathway early because it affects the scope of the question, the evidence we need, the evaluation plan, and the final deliverable. The exact requirements are set by the relevant graduate program.

## How we choose a topic

I generally do not expect a new MSc student to identify a novel research problem immediately. We normally begin with a broad research direction. You first build background knowledge by reading, reproducing, or experimenting with existing methods. From there, we identify limitations, unanswered questions, or opportunities for improvement.

A good topic should be researchable, sufficiently novel when a thesis contribution is expected, technically feasible, appropriately scoped for an MSc degree, and aligned with the available data and computing resources.

One common mistake is to start with the statement, "I found an interesting idea, so this will be my thesis." An interesting idea is only a starting point. I want us to understand what has already been done, what remains unresolved, and how we will test whether the proposed approach addresses a meaningful gap or need.

## A typical path

Broad area -> Initial reading -> Identify problems and gaps -> Discuss candidate directions -> Small exploratory experiments -> Refine research question -> Project or thesis proposal
