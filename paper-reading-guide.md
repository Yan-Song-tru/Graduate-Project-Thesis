# Research Paper Reading Guide

Reading research papers is a core skill for graduate students and anyone who works for a research project, and it is often not obvious how to read them efficiently without getting lost in technical detail. A useful strategy is to read each paper in stages.

## Step 1: Read the title, abstract, and introduction
Ask:

- What problem is this paper trying to solve?
- What is the main contribution?
- Why does the problem matter?

This gives you the big picture before reading the technical details.

## Step 2: Look at the method and evaluation quickly
Focus on:

- What data or task is being used?
- What method or model is proposed?
- What are the main baselines or comparisons?
- What metrics are used to measure success?

This helps you judge whether it is relevant to your own project.

## Step 3: Evaluate the contribution critically
Ask:

- What is novel?
- What is missing or unclear?
- Are the assumptions realistic?
- What are the main limitations?
- How does this paper relate to my own project or question?

## Step 4: Keep a reading summary
For each paper, record:

- Title and authors
- Why this paper matters
- Main research problem
- Method or approach
- Key strengths
- Key limitations
- How it connects to your own work

## Questions to ask while reading

- What problem does this paper solve?
- What data or method does it use?
- What are the main findings?
- What are the strongest limitations?
- Why does this paper matter for my own project?

## Good practice

- Read a few papers before choosing a direction.
- Do not try to understand every detail in the first pass.
- Focus on the idea before the equations.
- Summarize papers in your own words to verify understanding.
