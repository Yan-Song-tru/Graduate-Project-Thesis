# 1. Graduate Student Supervision

I supervise graduate students in areas related to video technology, computer vision, machine learning, deep learning, and data science applications. In this guide, I explain how I approach supervision, what I am currently interested in, what I ask from students, and how we typically move from an initial idea to an MSc project or thesis proposal.

For prospective students, the goal is to make the fit and the process easier to assess before you apply or contact me. For current and newly admitted students, the guide provides shared language for planning research, preparing for meetings, and moving toward a proposal.

# 2. My Supervision Philosophy

At the beginning of a project, I usually provide more structure and guidance. I may help you identify a research direction, choose foundational papers, define initial experiments, and establish a workable research routine. As you become more familiar with the area, I expect you to take increasing ownership: identifying problems, proposing solutions, designing experiments, and evaluating results critically.

I see graduate research as a collaboration. I bring research experience, feedback, direction, and support; you bring curiosity, preparation, persistence, and increasing ownership of the work. My role is not to give you every next step, but to help you learn how to determine the next step yourself.

> My role is not to give you every next step, but to help you learn how to determine the next step yourself.

This approach means that the amount of guidance you receive may change over time. Needing more structure at the beginning is normal. Becoming more independent is one of the main purposes of graduate research.
