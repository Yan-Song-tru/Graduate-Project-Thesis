# 9. Undergraduate Research Assistants

Undergraduate research assistant (RA) work is an opportunity to learn how research is carried out and contribute to an ongoing investigation. My work focuses on machine learning, deep learning, computer vision, and video understanding. Please read the [research interests](research.md) page to see whether these areas match your interests.

## What research assistant work involves

If your experience so far has been with course projects or building applications, research may feel unfamiliar. Building a working tool by calling APIs and integrating existing software can be useful engineering work. In a research RA role, however, a working implementation is only part of the task. You also need to understand the question being investigated, why a method might work, how to evaluate it fairly, and what the results actually show.

For example, an application might call an API to label a video. A research task might investigate when a video model fails, compare its performance with a baseline, or test whether a change helps when training labels are limited. That requires reading, controlled experiments, and analysis as well as coding.

Depending on your preparation and the project, responsibilities may include:

- Reading papers and explaining their main ideas, methods, and limitations.
- Preparing, checking, annotating, or exploring image and video datasets.
- Reproducing an existing method or establishing a baseline for comparison.
- Implementing, training, debugging, and evaluating machine learning models.
- Running controlled experiments and investigating errors or unexpected results.
- Keeping clear records of code, settings, data versions, and findings so that others can reproduce the work.
- Discussing progress and contributing to reports or other research outputs.

Research does not come with a guaranteed solution or a complete step-by-step recipe. An experiment that does not improve performance can still be useful if it is conducted carefully and helps us understand the problem. You are not expected to arrive ready to lead an independent research project, but you should be willing to move beyond following tutorials and explain your own work.

## Duration and learning expectations

**Please plan for a commitment of at least 6–8 months.** The specific minimum duration will be agreed before you begin. A short placement of only a few weeks is generally not enough for the learning and sustained contribution this work requires.

Deep learning and computer vision have a steep learning curve. Depending on your background, learning the basics and becoming comfortable with the research tools can take several months. Early work may therefore involve substantial reading, guided exercises, reproducing results, and debugging before you can contribute more independently.

Calendar duration alone is not enough: you also need consistent time each week. In your application, describe your realistic weekly availability, course load, proposed start date, and any planned breaks. We will discuss weekly hours, milestones, meeting arrangements, and how the role fits with your studies before you start.

## Eligibility and preparation

I look for undergraduate students who have:

- **Proficiency in Python:** you should be able to write, understand, and debug code independently, use packages, and work with existing codebases.
- **Familiarity with relevant Python packages:** for example, NumPy for numerical work, pandas for handling data, and Matplotlib for inspecting results.
- **Foundational machine learning knowledge:** including training, validation, and test sets; overfitting; and basic model evaluation.
- **A strong interest in learning research methods:** including reading technical material, working through uncertainty, and revising ideas in response to evidence.
- **The ability to make a sustained commitment:** with reliable communication and steady progress alongside coursework.

The following are additional strengths, rather than requirements that every applicant must already meet:

- Experience with a machine learning project or research placement.
- Experience training and evaluating neural networks, particularly with PyTorch or a similar framework.
- Experience working with images, videos, or other visual data, including tools such as OpenCV.
- Familiarity with Git, command-line tools, Linux, or GPU computing.
- Relevant coursework in linear algebra, probability, statistics, or optimization.

Course projects and independent learning can demonstrate your preparation. Explain what you personally implemented, how you evaluated it, and what you learned. Please distinguish between experience using an existing model through an API and experience training or evaluating models yourself; both are useful context for assessing your starting point.

## Working expectations and support

We will agree on initial tasks that match your preparation and the project's needs. I will provide direction, recommend relevant material, and give feedback. You should also be prepared to learn independently, try reasonable approaches, and ask for help with a clear description of what you have tried.

I expect you to:

- Attend agreed meetings and bring a brief update on progress, findings, obstacles, and next steps.
- Raise difficulties or changes in availability early.
- Keep code and experiment records organized, and report results honestly, including unsuccessful experiments.
- Follow project instructions for data access, privacy, storage, and use of shared computing resources.
- Acknowledge sources and collaborators, and understand and check any code or analysis produced with AI assistance.
- Leave usable documentation and a clear handover of your work when the appointment ends.

Before accepting a role, we should clarify the task scope, supervision arrangements, hours, compensation or funding, and expected duration. Opportunities depend on project needs and available resources. Research participation does not guarantee a publication; contributions and appropriate credit should be discussed as the work develops.

## How to apply

**Email me at [ysong@tru.ca](mailto:ysong@tru.ca) to request the undergraduate RA online application form, then complete and submit the form provided.** A suggested email subject is “Undergraduate RA inquiry — [Your name]”.

In your email, briefly include:

- Your program and year of study.
- Why you are interested in my research areas.
- Your Python and machine learning background, with a short example of relevant work or a link if available.
- Your proposed start date, expected weekly availability, and whether you can commit for at least 6–8 months.

The completed form will help me assess your preparation, interests, availability, and fit with current opportunities.
