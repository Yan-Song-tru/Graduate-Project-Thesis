# 6. How I Approach Literature Reviews and Proposals

I see the literature review as the bridge between an area of interest and a defensible research or project plan. The process is similar for both options, but the purpose is different: a thesis review should establish a research gap and motivate a novel contribution, while a project review should establish the practical need, inform the design or implementation, and justify how the result will be evaluated.

## Stage 1 - Learn the field

Start with surveys or review papers and several representative or foundational papers. I want you to learn the terminology, major tasks, commonly used datasets, evaluation metrics, and major families of methods. At this stage, focus on building a broad map of the field rather than trying to read everything.

## Stage 2 - Build a structured literature review

Rather than collecting isolated summaries, organize the literature around comparable dimensions. A useful review records, where relevant:

- Research problem
- Method
- Dataset
- Supervision or annotation setting
- Strengths
- Limitations
- Results
- Remaining research gaps

A literature-review spreadsheet or template can make patterns, disagreements, and gaps easier to compare. I am looking for an analytical review that helps us decide why a direction is worth pursuing, not simply a record showing that papers have been read.

## Stage 3 - Connect the review to your pathway

### Thesis option

For a thesis, the review should identify an unresolved research gap and show how the proposed work could make a sufficiently novel contribution. You should be able to explain what is missing from the literature, why that gap matters, and what research question or hypothesis you will investigate.

### Project option

For a project, the review should clarify the problem or practical need, compare relevant existing approaches, and provide a reasoned basis for the system, implementation, adaptation, or evaluation you propose. The project does not necessarily need to make the same level of novel research contribution as a thesis, but it should have a clear objective, appropriate scope, and defensible evaluation plan.

## Questions your proposal should answer

Regardless of pathway, I would expect you to be able to answer these questions clearly:

- What problem or need am I addressing?
- Why is it important?
- What has already been done?
- What limitation, opportunity, or requirement motivates this work?
- What do I propose to investigate, build, compare, or evaluate?
- How will I determine whether the outcome is successful?

For a thesis, these answers should form a clear argument for novelty and research contribution. For a project, they should form a clear argument for relevance, feasibility, design choices, and practical or technical value. In both cases, the proposal should grow out of the literature review rather than beginning with an unsupported idea.
